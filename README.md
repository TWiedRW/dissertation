# Overview

This dissertation is about the graphical perception of 3D-printed charts. 
Our main goal is to provide validity to the use of true 3D charts, which currently have novel uses in the realm of data visualization.
The theory is that truly 3-dimensional charts are better than 3D charts rendered on 2D screens.

## Project 1: Bar charts
The initial project partially replicated a 1984 paper by Cleveland and McGill. 
We took some of the charts used by Cleveland and McGill and created 3D-printed versions.
The experiment ask participants two questions: (1) what marked bar is smaller, and (2) what is the size of the smaller marked bar with respect to the larger bar.
Participants were recruited from the department, but most participants came from Stat 218 sections.

## Project 2: Stat 218 Experiential Learning
A secondary goal of Project 1 and Project 3 is to provide students with an experiental learning opportunity in their introductory statistics course.
The main benefit of using an experiment on 3D visualizations is that it is an approachable statistics topic.
We ask students to read a series of reflections at various stages of the experiment:
- pre-experiment: asking students their initial views on science in general
- post-experiment: asking students what they thought the study was measuring
- abstract: students read and reflect on an abstract about the design of the experiment
- presentation: students watch and reflect on a video presentation about the results of the experiment

## Project 3: Heat maps
The final project is an extension of Project 1, where heatmaps are used instead of bar charts.
The stimuli of the experiment are different from Cleveland and McGill, but the questions asked are the same.
